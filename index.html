01 / ACCEPTED TO IROS 2026 · FIRST-LISTED CO-FIRST AUTHOR

RoboHarness

A memory-augmented policy harness for VLA robustness

Vision-language-action models can execute diverse manipulation skills, yet frozen policies remain brittle under perceptual noise, language ambiguity, execution failures, and long-horizon distribution shifts. RoboHarness adds adaptation outside the policy: contrastive Dual-Memory RAG retrieves both successful and failed precedents, while an attribution-driven multimodal orchestrator diagnoses the current failure and selects tool-mediated interventions without updating model parameters.

Offline memory consolidation turns execution traces into reusable priors. Across π0, π0.5, and SmolVLA on LIBERO-PRO and LIBERO-RoboHarness, the harness delivers a 56.6-percentage-point average absolute success-rate gain, including 89.1 points on long-horizon task chaining. The result frames robot adaptation as a modular systems layer around existing policies.

02 / ONGOING RESEARCH · CO-AUTHOR

ManiLadder

A multi-level benchmark for manipulation intelligence

Robot manipulation benchmarks often isolate a single capability or rely on ad hoc tasks, making it difficult to answer a basic question: how difficult a task can a policy actually solve? ManiLadder studies manipulation intelligence as a staged progression across more than one hundred simulated tasks and four levels of difficulty.

A modular workflow connects task construction, demonstration collection, policy training, and automated evaluation so imitation-learning and VLA policies can be compared under one repeatable protocol. Rather than reducing performance to one aggregate score, the benchmark is designed to show where capability changes as tasks require longer horizons, richer coordination, and stronger generalization. The project remains ongoing; public paper, code, and media will be linked after the team release.

03 / ICML 2026 REGULAR PAPER · CO-AUTHOR

GKD-Recruiter

Graph knowledge distillation for spatial crowdsourcing

Spatial crowdsourcing platforms can recruit through social networks, but conventional influence maximization assumes that interest becomes participation and that more spread is always better. Both assumptions fail when workers must physically reach heterogeneous tasks and each task has finite demand. GKD-Recruiter instead optimizes Effective Task Satisfaction through a heterogeneous graph of worker–task affinity.

An Influential GAT captures directional social influence, graph knowledge distillation fuses it with task-side signals, and Rainbow DQN searches the resulting non-submodular recruitment space. Experiments on real-world datasets show significant gains over state-of-the-art baselines in solution quality and inference efficiency. The framework makes recruitment task-aware: selected workers must not only propagate invitations, but satisfy the right physical tasks under limited demand.

04 / ECML PKDD 2025 · FIRST AUTHOR

DRNCS

Dual-level route generation through contraction and shortcuts

Learning-based route generation becomes increasingly expensive on long routes, where dense road graphs enlarge the autoregressive search space and compromise real-time service. DRNCS introduces a dual-level route model built around Shortcut-Edge Differential Contraction: nodes are removed according to edge difference to avoid the out-degree explosion common in standard contraction schemes, producing a much sparser graph while preserving useful structure.

Likely shortcuts are constructed by merging historical routes with probability-weighted bidirectional Dijkstra search, then invoked while the model predicts on the contracted graph. Across five real-world datasets, DRNCS reports 0.3–0.8 milliseconds per trip, 1,249–3,356 generated trips per second, and more than 40% faster queries while maintaining competitive accuracy and reachability.

05 / TCS 1054 (2025) · CO-AUTHOR

Algorithms for Shortest Path Tour Problem

Exact algorithms for ordered waypoints on road networks

Carpooling with multiple candidate pickup and drop-off locations leads to the Shortest Path Tour Problem: find an origin-to-destination path that visits an ordered sequence of disjoint node subsets while choosing one suitable node from each. Existing exact methods can spend substantial time materializing or searching expanded structures.

This work develops two exact, linear-space alternatives. Stage Dijkstra solves the problem one subset at a time and attains the best-known O(ℓ(n+m) log n) time bound; Global Dijkstra explores multiple stages concurrently and runs faster in practice on large road networks. Experiments on real and synthetic graphs confirm strong time and memory efficiency, with applications beyond carpooling to ordered multi-stage tasks.
